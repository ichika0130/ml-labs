## Problem

In most games, NPC (non-player character) dialogue is written by hand and does not react to what the player actually did. Large language models could generate reactive dialogue, but they are far too big and slow to ship inside a game. This project builds a **small, local model that generates an NPC's line when the player interacts with it**, conditioned on:

- the **NPC's personality** (e.g. greedy, cowardly, loyal, arrogant),
- the **player's recent action** toward the NPC (e.g. gave a gift, attacked, stole, completed its quest),
- the **relationship** between them (hostile, neutral, friendly).

Example: personality = *greedy*, action = *gave a gift*, relationship = *neutral* → "Oh? For me? Well... I suppose you're not as useless as you look."

The model must run offline on a laptop CPU within the limits below. The question is how much a very small model can still respect these conditions, and what quantization and distillation cost.

## Dataset

There is no public dataset that pairs NPC lines with personality and player-action labels, so the dataset is **generated by a large teacher model** and then **distilled** into the small model.

- **Labels (fixed sets):** about 8 personalities × 10 player actions × 3 relationship levels = 240 combinations. The final label lists are fixed before generation.
- **Generation:** an open-weight instruction-tuned model of about 7 B parameters (e.g. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct), run locally. For each combination it receives a fixed prompt with a short setting description (a dark-fantasy world written by me) and generates about 20–30 short lines (≤ 30 words). Target size: about 5,000–7,000 lines. Prompts, model name and sampling settings are logged so the dataset can be regenerated.
- **Cleaning:** remove duplicates and near-duplicates, lines that are too long or not in English, and lines that simply name the label (e.g. "I am greedy"). A random sample of 100 lines is checked by hand and the share of bad lines is reported.
- **Splits:**
  - random split of lines (80 / 10 / 10);
  - an **unseen-combination** test set: about 10% of the 240 label combinations never appear in training, to test whether the model can combine personality and action in a new way.
- **Input format:** label tokens followed by the line, e.g. `<greedy> <gift> <neutral> | Oh? For me? ...`. The labels are special tokens, so they cost almost nothing in the vocabulary.

Using generated data avoids the copyright and labelling problems of real game text. Its limitation, that the style comes from the teacher model, is discussed in the report.

## Success metrics

| Metric | What it measures |
|---|---|
| Test perplexity (seen and unseen combinations) | How well the model predicts held-out lines |
| **Condition accuracy** | A separate small classifier, trained on the teacher data, predicts personality, action and relationship from a line. Applied to generated lines, it shows whether the model follows the requested conditions. Its accuracy on real teacher lines is the upper bound |
| Distinct-2, repetition rate, copy rate | Whether lines are varied, do not loop, and are not just copied from the training data |
| Blind rating of 60 lines (1–5) | "Fits the personality", "fits the player's action", "makes sense"; lines from all model versions and the teacher are shuffled before rating |

**Target:** the deployable model meets both constraints below and keeps at least 90% of the reference model's condition accuracy.

## Deployment constraints

| Constraint | Limit | How it is measured |
|---|---|---|
| Latency | **≤ 50 ms per generated token** | Median over 200 generated tokens, batch 1, laptop CPU, after warm-up, with a key-value cache. The time for a full line (≈ 30 tokens) is reported as well |
| Model size | **≤ 5 MB** | Size of the exported model file on disk |

5 MB allows about 1.2 M parameters in float32 or about 5 M in INT8, so the vocabulary must stay small (character-level or a BPE vocabulary of about 1,000 tokens plus the label tokens).

## Planned model and optimisation

- **Model:** a decoder-only Transformer (GPT-style) written from scratch in PyTorch, trained with the YAML/MLflow pipeline from Lab 6 (seed, checkpoints, logged runs).
- **Distillation, step 1 (sequence-level):** the 7 B teacher's knowledge is transferred through the generated dataset.
- **Reference model:** a small GPT of about 5–6 M parameters (about 20–24 MB in float32) trained on this data. It does **not** meet the size limit and shows the best quality reachable at this scale.
- **Optimisation 1, quantization:** INT8 post-training quantization of the reference model (about 4× smaller), exported to ONNX Runtime.
- **Optimisation 2, distillation step 2 (logit-level):** a student of about 1 M parameters trained on the reference model's output probabilities, then quantized.
- **Comparison:** reference, INT8 reference, student, INT8 student (and the 7 B teacher for quality only) on all metrics, size and latency.

## Risks

- Lines generated by one teacher model may be repetitive or generic; cleaning, the hand-checked sample and the distinct-n metric make this visible.
- A small model may ignore rare combinations; the unseen-combination test set measures this directly.
- The condition classifier can be wrong itself; its accuracy on teacher lines is reported so its results can be interpreted.
- INT8 quantization of very small Transformers can cost noticeable quality. If it does, only the linear layers are quantized and embeddings and layer norms stay in float32.
